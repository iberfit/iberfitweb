IBERFIT IRI Assessment

Before we tell you what to train, we want to understand where you are today.

This is not an exam and you do not need to arrive fit. It is a conversation and an assessment to understand where we should begin.

We look at your context, how you move and how you respond to effort so the first step makes sense for you.

An assessment you can discuss

Data matters when you can understand it.

The initial assessment brings together context, body composition and physical capabilities. It is then interpreted with you and turned into practical priorities.

Bioimpedance provides indicative information and is interpreted alongside the rest of the assessment.

What it assesses

You are not here to prove anything. We are here to understand what you need.

01

Context

Goals, experience, availability, relevant background and training environment.

02

Body composition

Useful measurements for establishing a baseline and reviewing change where appropriate.

03

Mobility

Observation of relevant movement to support exercise selection and adaptation.

04

Strength

Pattern-based assessment to identify a functional baseline and development priorities.

05

Conditioning

Effort response and recovery using tests appropriate to the individual context.

What you receive

You receive a clear link between findings, priorities and next steps.

  • A clear summary of the starting point.
  • Initial priorities—not a permanent classification.
  • Recommended format and level of support.
  • A reference point for future review.

Normal questions before the IRI

What happens in the assessment, and what happens afterwards.

We want you to arrive knowing what we will look at, why it matters and how we use that information with you.

What exactly is the IRI Assessment?

It is a structured initial assessment designed to understand your starting point and what should be prioritised. It brings together context, measurements and physical capacities and ends with practical guidance.

What does the IRI assess?

It considers context, body composition, mobility, strength and conditioning when relevant. Tests are only included when the information can support a useful decision.

What do I receive afterwards?

You receive a clear reading of your baseline, initial priorities and a recommendation on training format and level of support. That baseline can support later comparisons when protocols are compatible.

Is the IRI the same as ongoing review?

No. The IRI is the initial assessment and entry point; ongoing review happens during training to evaluate progress and adjust the plan.

Is bioimpedance a medical diagnosis?

No. IBERFIT uses bioimpedance as a contextual reference, never as a medical diagnosis or an isolated conclusion.
